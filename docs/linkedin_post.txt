HOOK:
🛞 Most drivers replace tires by time or feel. What if your car knew exactly how much tread was left — without any extra hardware?

POST:
Worn tires are a leading cause of road accidents. EU regulations (ECE-R30) set a 1.6 mm minimum tread depth, but tires should ideally be replaced at 3 mm for wet performance.

The problem: standard vehicles don't have direct tread sensors. But they do have a CAN bus full of data.

In this project, I built a Random Forest model that predicts remaining tread depth (mm) from 10 CAN-derived features:
→ Total mileage
→ Average speed & acceleration patterns
→ ABS activation frequency (aggressive braking proxy)
→ Average cornering G (lateral load)
→ Tire pressure (under-inflation accelerates wear)
→ Vehicle mass & axle load distribution

Results:
✅ R² = 0.993 on 400 unseen test samples
✅ RMSE = 0.18 mm
✅ CV R² = 0.993 ± 0.001

Key finding: Mileage + aggressive braking events account for ~70% of explained variance — but cornering intensity and pressure are the hidden amplifiers.

Application: Zero-hardware predictive alert integrated directly into the vehicle's existing TPMS module.
